EFFECT OF A STRUCTURED SELF-CARE EDUCATION PROGRAM ON QUALITY OF LIFE IN HEMODIALYSIS PATIENTS: A RANDOMIZED CONTROLLED TRIAL

ABSTRACT
Background: Hemodialysis patients experience reduced quality of life. Aim: To evaluate the effect of a structured self-care education program on quality of life. Methods: In this randomized controlled trial, 68 hemodialysis patients were randomly assigned to intervention (n=34) and control (n=34) groups. The intervention group received six weekly self-care education sessions. Quality of life was measured using KDQOL-SF at baseline and after 12 weeks. Data were analyzed using independent t-test and paired t-test with SPSS 26. Results: Mean quality of life score increased in the intervention group (from 45.2±12.3 to 58.7±13.1) compared with control (from 44.8±11.9 to 46.1±12.4) (p<0.001, mean difference 12.2, 95% CI: 8.1 to 16.3). Conclusion: Structured self-care education improved quality of life in hemodialysis patients.

Keywords: hemodialysis, self-care, quality of life, education, randomized controlled trial

INTRODUCTION
Chronic kidney disease is a major public health problem worldwide. Patients undergoing hemodialysis face numerous physical and psychological challenges that reduce their quality of life (1). Previous studies have shown that educational interventions may improve self-care behaviors and clinical outcomes (2). However, few studies have examined structured self-care education programs in small cities of Iran. The aim of this study was to determine the effect of a structured self-care education program on quality of life of hemodialysis patients.

METHODS
Study design and setting: This randomized controlled trial was conducted in the dialysis center of Behbahan from 2024 to 2025. The trial was approved by the ethics committee (IR.BMSU.REC.1403.045) and registered in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT2024050012345N1).
Participants: Inclusion criteria: age >18 years, at least 3 months on hemodialysis, informed consent. Exclusion criteria: malignancy, cognitive impairment.
Randomization and blinding: Patients were randomly assigned using block randomization with blocks of four. The outcome assessor was blinded to group allocation.
Intervention: The intervention group received six weekly education sessions (60 minutes each) covering fluid management, diet, medication adherence, and vascular access care. The control group received routine care.
Outcome: Quality of life was measured using the Persian version of Kidney Disease Quality of Life Short Form (KDQOL-SF), which has been validated in Iranian population.
Sample size: Based on mean difference of 10 points, SD=15, alpha=0.05, power=80%, 30 patients per group were required; 34 were recruited to account for attrition.
Statistical analysis: Data were analyzed using SPSS 26. Independent t-test, paired t-test, and chi-square test were used. p<0.05 was considered significant. Intent-to-treat analysis was not performed. Normality was assessed by Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Missing data were handled by excluding incomplete cases.

RESULTS
Of 80 patients assessed for eligibility, 68 were randomized (34 per group). Twelve patients were excluded: 6 did not meet criteria, 6 declined. During follow-up, 4 patients in intervention and 3 in control group were lost. Baseline characteristics were similar between groups (Table 1): mean age 55.3±14.2 vs 54.1±15.0 years, 52.9% vs 50.0% female (p>0.05).
After 12 weeks, quality of life improved significantly in the intervention group (45.2±12.3 to 58.7±13.1) compared with control (44.8±11.9 to 46.1±12.4); between-group difference in change was 12.2 (95% CI: 8.1 to 16.3, p<0.001, Cohen's d=0.95).

DISCUSSION
This study showed that a structured self-care education program significantly improved quality of life in hemodialysis patients. Our findings are consistent with previous studies (2,3). The effect size was large. This study proves that self-care education is the best intervention for dialysis patients everywhere. Limitations include single-center design and 12-week follow-up.

CONCLUSION
Structured self-care education improves quality of life of hemodialysis patients and should be incorporated into routine dialysis care.

ETHICS
The study was approved by the ethics committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. No conflicts of interest declared. The dataset is available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.
